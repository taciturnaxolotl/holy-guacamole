# Wheel tread mould (Ø42 × 12, VytaFlex 40 on an MJF PA11 hub; v3.4h, was × 9)

**Parts**
- `mould_base.stl` locates the hub on its bore pin and seals the rim face.
- `mould_ring.stl` forms the tread OD (Ø42) and sits in the base's register step.
- `mould_lid.stl` seals the top. It has a Ø4 pour hole and a Ø2 vent, and clears the bearing stub.

**Print:** PETG or PLA on FDM, 0.12–0.16 mm layers, and sand the ring bore smooth. Layer lines transfer straight onto the tread.

**Cast:**
1. Put mould release (e.g. Ease Release 200) on the mould parts only. Don't get any on the hub: the tread holds by the two dovetails and the 12 ribs.
2. Mix 5.0 cc of tread per wheel and pour about 6.5 cc to allow for the sprue (about 5.0 g of VytaFlex 40 per tread). Degas if you can, or pour slowly down one side.
3. Fill until resin shows at the vent.
4. Demould at VytaFlex's stated demould time. The ring slides off axially.
5. Trim the sprue flush.
6. Cast a spare or two: small treads are cheap, and it is easier to scrap one with bubbles than to chase them.

**Check each wheel before it goes on** (from Discord experience on ant and beetle meltys; the treads see about 1000 g at 4000 rpm, which pushes each 5.0 g tread about 50 N along the axle, toward the outboard bearing):
- **Rib roots:** look through the tread at the dovetails and ribs. Bubbles there are what let a tread walk off; bubbles elsewhere are cosmetic.
- **Axial push:** hold the hub and push the tread hard along the axle both ways with your thumbs. It must not creep or lift.
- **Squeeze:** pinch round the tread; hard and soft spots mean voids.
- **Bearing seat:** no urethane skin in the stub or the bearing seat.
- **Weigh:** the pair within about 0.2 g of each other (about 8.5 g each with the hub).

Expect wear: narrow treads go fastest (9–10 mm T40s shredded in a fight; ~25 mm cast 40A lasted about 4). Keep spares at events, and if a hub gets hot enough to soften after long spins with braking, that is the thing to fix next.
